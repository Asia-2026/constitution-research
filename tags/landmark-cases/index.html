    B. R. Ambedkar and the Surprising Call to Speak (17 December 1946)#

    On 17 December 194G, in the midst of intense and formative deliberations of the Constituent Assembly of India, Dr B. R. Ambedkar found himself speaking under entirely unexpected circumstances. The debate centred on the “Objectives Resolution” introduced by Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 December, which laid out the guiding aims and principles for the future Constitution of India.     [Indira Gandhi versus Raj Narain]{.underline} is one of the most, if not the most, momentous cases in post-independent Indian history, which brought a plethora of changes in Indian politics as well as society. The case dates back to 1971, when Raj Narain, a socialist leader, accused Prime Minister Indira Gandhi of malpractices in the general election of 1971 and moved a petition in the Allahabad High Court to declare the election null and void.     MANEKA GANDHI VS. THE UNION OF INDIA#

    [Maneka Gandhi versus the Union of India]{.underline} is a landmark case that provided a new interpretation of fundamental rights. The case dates back to 1978. Maneka Gandhi, on receiving a notice from the Regional Passport Officer to surrender her passport and not finding any legitimate reason to do so, filed a writ petition under Article 32. She argued that the notice violates her right of Personal Liberty accorded in Article 21 of the Indian Constitution. A bench of 7 judges was formed for the hearing of the petition.

    Upholding the sanctity of the Constitution: Basic Structure Doctrine

    1. Shankari Prasad Singh Deo v. Union of India (1951)

    Shankari Prasad, a West Bengal zamindar, challenged the First Amendment Act, 1951, which curtailed the right to property to facilitate the abolition of zamindari and land redistribution. The Supreme Court held that constitutional amendments are not "laws" under Article 13(2), meaning amendments fall outside the restriction on laws protecting fundamental rights. This gave Parliament unlimited power to amend any provision, including fundamental rights. This case removed legal barriers to agrarian reforms and set the precedent for absolute parliamentary amendment power.​
